Spanish Copy Audit · Project

When a card fails, your Spanish email gets one chance.

We audit and rewrite your customer-facing Spanish (failed-payment emails, renewal reminders, cancellation flows, support macros) so it reads as written for your customers, not run through a translator. Every change comes with the reason behind it.

Involuntary churn is a copy problem

A customer whose card fails hasn't decided to leave. The email decides for them. If it reads like a machine translation, it reads like a scam, and nobody updates their card details for a scam.

What we audit

  • Failed-payment and dunning emails
  • Renewal and price-change notices
  • Cancellation and retention flows, in-app and on the web
  • Chat and support macros
  • Help-center articles and key website pages

Original, rewrite, and why

Every change is shown side by side with the reasoning. Corrected text on its own is a commodity. The reasoning is what tells your team how to write the next one.

“Declinada”
An anglicism for “declined”. It's common in some markets and jarring in others. Rephrasing avoids it everywhere.
“Tu tarjeta fue declinada”
Puts the failure on the customer. “No pudimos procesar el pago” takes it on the company, which matters in a message about money.
“¡Oops!”
Reads as flippant to many Latin American customers when it's about a failed charge.
“Información de facturación”
Vague. “Tu tarjeta” says exactly what to update.

About the Retention Audit

Which Spanish?

Latin American Spanish, adjusted for your main markets. If one version won't serve all of them, we'll tell you where it breaks.

Tú or usted?

We'll recommend one for your brand and customers, then apply it consistently. Mixing the two is a common sign of translated copy.
